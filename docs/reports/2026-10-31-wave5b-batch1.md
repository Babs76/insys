# Wave 5b batch 5b.1: pharmacy claims (F039 to F045) and what a principal member sees (OQ-161)

Built to `docs/specs/addenda/benefit-programmes.md`, signed by Ade on 31 October 2026.

## What it does
- A claim format with `line_kind` `PHARMACY` takes pharmacy lines and says which fields they need. A line is checked at validation; its `tariff_code` holds the product code.
- The engine adds steps for pharmacy lines only: formulary and tier, the prescription, the maximum quantity and days' supply, the reference price, the tier's co-payment and limit. Deductible, accumulators, approval and runs are the existing ones. A claim with no pharmacy line reads nothing new.
- A medicine is clinical. Staff views show `PHARMACY` in place of the code; `GET /health/claims/{id}/clinical` shows it, for the clinical function, with a purpose, audited.
- OQ-161 applies to all claims from now on: a principal member's portal view hides the code on an adult dependant's claim. A minor's stays visible; anyone sees their own.

## Choices for Ade to know
- **What a limit has used is counted from paid claims, not stored.** A reversal gives it back with no separate step, so `chronic_entitlement` will not need the addendum's "used to date" column and `preventive_use` may not be needed.
- **Hidden from the principal: every line's code on an adult dependant's claim**, not only medicines, since a procedure code reveals a diagnosis. The principal still sees the provider, date and amounts, and a provider's name can hint at a condition. *For compliance to confirm with OQ-161.*
- **A dependant is an adult from 18.** One whose birth date can't be read counts as an adult.
- **A prior-authorised medicine needs a category mapping for the provider type**, because the preauthorisation path maps codes through `cfg_benefit_category_mappings`, not the formulary.
- `reference_group` is recorded but not used: OQ-152 doesn't require generics.

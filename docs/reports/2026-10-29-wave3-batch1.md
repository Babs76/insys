## Partitioned tables (Ade's question 1)

| Table | Partitioned on | What sets it | Can it be in the future? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `integration.external_call_log` | `called_at` | The clock, when the call is made | No |
| `rating.rating_request` | `calculated_at` | The clock, when it is calculated (the as-at rating date is a separate column) | No |
| `rules.decision_log` | `evaluated_at` | The clock | No |
| `document.document_access_log` | `accessed_at` | The clock | No |
| `health.eligibility_decision` | `decided_at` | The clock | No |
| `audit.audit_event` | `occurred_at` | The clock | No |
| `finance.journal`, `finance.subledger_entry` | `accounting_date` | Postings take the business date the event happened on, which is today in every handler. A reversal and a retried posting take a date the user names, within an open period. | **Yes:** a period can be opened any distance ahead |

- **The fix:** opening an accounting period first creates its months' partitions through `finance.ensure_ledger_partitions` (V0089). This is security definer, granted to the Finance role, and limited to 36 months per call. Every date a posting can take is therefore covered.
- **The test:** a period opened 20 months ahead (past every partition the test database has) gets its partitions, and a reversal dated in it posts.
- **No table keys on a cover start, a policy's effective date or a rating date.**

## Found by the fixed clocks
- **Defect, cessions by month.** `ReinsuranceListCessions` with `periodCode`, and the cessions a bordereau takes, used UTC months. A cession at 23:30 UTC on 31 March, which is 1 April in Johannesburg, fell into March's bordereau. Both now use the tenant's business month (ADR-025). Found on the 23:30 UTC clock.
- **A test assumption, not a defect.** Health's flow took one benefit accumulator per scope. A preauthorisation valid from 40 days ago reserves in the benefit period containing that date, which near a year end is the previous one. The check now names the period.

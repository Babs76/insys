1. **BR-013 (defect), first:** a new tenant is configured and activated by its own people.
   - The first-administrator request names exactly two people. A second platform administrator approves it; no platform administrator approves anything inside the tenant (V0062, `iam.tenant_administrator_person`).
   - `FIRST_ADMINISTRATOR` readiness needs two active administrators: an active `TENANT_ADMIN` assignment, the identity linked, and one successful signed-in request each (OQ-119).
   - `POST /identity/tenant-administrators/additional`: one more administrator for an existing tenant, asked for by a platform operator and approved by a second platform administrator.
   - **Found while building the end-to-end test, for your confirmation (OQ-119):** two administrators were still not enough. Workflow needs an authority row for every approval (WFL-004), a new tenant has none, and adding one is itself a Tier 1 change. A privileged role assignment could not be approved by anyone in the tenant. **Built:** an administrator the platform provisions raises `iam.AdministratorGranted` in its own tenant, and Workflow then puts `TENANT_ADMIN`'s authority in force there (`PRIVILEGED_ROLE`, `EXTERNAL_SUBJECT_LINK`, `STATIC_DATA_T1`, `STATIC_DATA_T2`, as `role-templates.md` lists it), only when the tenant has no authority row for the role. Workflow does not act as another tenant; the event is consumed in the new tenant.
   - **End-to-end test** (`ATenant_IsCreatedConfiguredAndActivated_UsingOnlyItsOwnPeople_WithThePrivilegedFlagsOn_BR013`), with the flags on: the operator registers the tenant, a second platform administrator approves its two administrators, and both sign in. The other administrator approves each change, and the maker is refused on their own. The steps complete and the tenant is activated. A third administrator is added through the platform path. The test checks that every approval in the tenant was decided by one of its own people (3 of 3).

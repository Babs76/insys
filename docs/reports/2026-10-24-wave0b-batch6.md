## 1. External-user access, the remainder
- **Portal documents** (`/portal/documents`): a portal user reads a document only when:
  - its business record is in their relationship, which the module owning the record answers (General policies and endorsements, Life policies, invoices, statements, remittances);
  - and its category's `allowed_roles` include one of their roles, whatever its classification.

  So each tenant chooses which categories its portals show, by naming portal roles on them; none shows by default. Every read is in the document's access log with purpose `PORTAL`.
- **Portal invoices:** a member's are those of the accounts they pay; a broker's, those of its policies and its sub-brokers' (General and Life tell Billing which policies a broker has). Both within F103 scope.
- **Portal statements:** a broker's approved and paid statements, and its sub-brokers'. Drafts are not shown.
- **Member claim views:**
  - **Life:** the claimant, the policyholder and a nominated beneficiary see the claim, and so does the policy's broker.
  - **Health:** the patient sees their claims, and the principal sees every claim of the membership; a dependant does not see the others' (**OQ-084**, for you to confirm). Neither view shows the assessment, reserves, decision reasons or diagnosis codes.
- **Party-match resolution:** staff propose LINK or NEW (`POST /party/parties/{id}/match-resolution`), and a second person approves `PARTY_MATCH_RESOLUTION` in Workflow.
  - A LINK merges the registration into the matched party, through the existing PTY-004 merge with its history.
  - NEW confirms it as a new party.
  - A rejection returns it to review.
  - Either way, the review task closes.
  - A broker quoting for a linked registration now quotes for the existing party.
- **Keycloak:**
  - **The portal clients** `broker-portal`, `provider-portal` and `member-portal`. Brokers and providers sign in with a password and a one-time code, always; members add a code once they have set one up.
  - **The `amr` mapper:** each step of those flows names its `amr` value.
  - **Checked** by importing the realm into Keycloak 26.7.5: a token carried `amr: ["pwd"]`.
  - **Redirect URIs** are the local development ones; each environment sets its portals' real URLs.
- **Tests (end to end):**
  - **Invoices:** member and both brokers.
  - **Documents:** category filtering, the relationship, and the access log.
  - **Statements.**
  - **Claims:** Life claimant, stranger and broker; Health patient, principal and another member.
  - **Party-match resolution:** maker cannot approve; LINK merges, closes the task, and the broker's quote goes to the existing party.
  - **Unit:** a NEW proposal and a rejection.

## 2. Request 46, the remainder: past the last bucket
- An open arrears case that ages past every bucket stays in its bucket with its true days overdue. On the day it leaves its bucket's range, it raises `billing.ArrearsAgeingBlocked` once.
- Billing's consumer parks that delivery (`ARREARS_AGEING_BUCKET_NOT_IN_FORCE`, with the days as item), which alerts as parked deliveries do (ADR-022). Once a bucket covering the days is published, the retry ages the case on.
- **The rule** that buckets cover every day from day 1 to the last action is in the tenant runbook (`tenants-and-access.md`) and in the onboarding readiness check `COLLECTION_METHODS`, which also refuses gaps, overlaps, and a last action that is not LAPSE or CANCEL.
- **Tests:**
  - parked once, not once a day;
  - the case moves on once a bucket is published;
  - the readiness check names an overlap.

## 4. Onboarding readiness (TEN-008)
- **How it works:** completing a step runs its checks in the tenant, on its business date. Any check not ready refuses the step with 422 `ONBOARDING_STEP_NOT_READY`, whose `errors` name each check and what is missing; nothing changes. `GET /tenancy/tenants/{id}/readiness` lists every step's checks.
- **Who answers:** each module checks the configuration it owns, on its own connection: a generic "static data in force" check, plus custom checks.
  - **Custom checks:** posting rules, an open period, approval authority, templates in the default language, bucket coverage, payment methods in the base currency, a provider with status mappings and a callback endpoint, channels per staff role, domain codes, sellable products with templates, and rating test cases that pass, run through the rater.
- **Waivers and evidence** go on the onboarding case (V0048): "no reinsurance" and "no webhooks" with a reason, and the end-to-end run's reference. A check that allows none refuses one with `ATTESTATION_NOT_ALLOWED`.
- **Tests:** a test tenant is configured by a schema-driven seed, and both onboarding test classes now onboard real configuration.
  - A step is refused with both failing checks named.
  - A waiver is required, and a wrong one refused.
  - The tenant activates, with every step completed and ready.

### Settled when building (your points)
- **Who performs each step, and in which tenant status:**
  - **Every step happens while the tenant is Onboarding.**
  - **The platform operator completes each step**, in order (`tenant.update` in the operator tenant). The exception is `ORGANISATION_AND_ACCESS`, which completes itself when the first administrator is approved, but now only if its other checks pass.
  - **The operator loads `PLATFORM_REFERENCE_DATA`** (global static data) and registers the tenant with its time zone.
  - **The tenant's first administrator and the users it adds load everything else** (locale defaults, roles, channels, finance, operations, payments, products) and run the end-to-end test.
- **What changes:** **the tenant's users may now sign in while it is Onboarding.** Before, they were refused until activation, which made the configuration impossible to load: the operator manages only global static data. Suspended and Terminated tenants are still refused, and the background jobs still run for Active tenants only. The first-administrator test's "403 before activation" now expects 200.
- **The open points, built as proposed:**
  - **`POSTING_RULES`:** only the transaction types the business raises.
  - **Waivers:** only `TREATIES` and `WEBHOOKS_OR_NONE`.
  - **`SMOKE_RUN`:** recorded by the operator, not automated.
- **Interpretations to confirm:**
  - **`DOMAIN_CODES`:** a domain counts as set up once its numbering scheme is in force, and then its own codes are required.
  - **`PRODUCT_TEMPLATES`:** a product's document types are not specified, so at least one template per sellable product is required.
  - **`BASE_CURRENCY`:** checks that the base currency is set. Exchange rates for other currencies are not checked, because products do not declare their currencies.

## 5. Keycloak user provisioning
- **What it does:**
  - **Provisioning:** a user of INSYS's own realm (`idpCode` `KEYCLOAK`) is provisioned with an email, or approved as a first administrator.
  - **Pending identity:** it is `PENDING_IDENTITY`, and cannot sign in, until Identity's consumer has created its Keycloak user and linked it, or linked the one a retry finds (same username and tenant).
  - **The invitation:** once linked, the user is Invited, and Keycloak sends the required-actions email as its own step.
  - **Lock and disable** disable the Keycloak user and end its sessions; unlock enables it again.
  - **When Keycloak fails:** Keycloak unreachable (`KEYCLOAK_UNAVAILABLE`), a username held by another tenant's user (`KEYCLOAK_USER_CONFLICT`), and an unsent email (`KEYCLOAK_EMAIL_UNSENT`) each park under ADR-022.
  - **The realm** gains the `insys-user-admin` service client, with `manage-users`, `view-users` and `query-users`. Its secret is generated by Keycloak and never in git.
- **Tests:** against Keycloak 26.7.5 in a container, with the committed realm export.
  - **Creation:** exactly one user with the right tenant; a replay creates none; a pre-existing user is linked; another tenant's username parks; Keycloak down parks, and the user is created on retry.
  - **Access:** a user with no roles signs in and can do nothing; disabling disables it in Keycloak.
  - **Secrets:** the secret never appears in logs.
  - **Unit:** the lifecycle.

### Settled when building (your points)
- **The username convention:** `<tenant code>.<username>`, in lower case (Keycloak keeps usernames in lower case). One realm serves every tenant, and usernames are unique per realm.
- **One person in several tenants:** one Keycloak user per tenant, each with its own `tenant_id`.
  - **Duplicate emails:** they may share an email, so the realm now allows duplicate emails. Keycloak then switches "Login with email" off itself. Checked on 26.7.5: the realm accepted `duplicateEmailsAllowed: true` with `loginWithEmailAllowed: true` and stored login-with-email as false.
  - **Sign-in** is therefore by username. The settings are in the Server Administration Guide's realm settings, under "Duplicate emails" and "Login with email"; the behaviour above is what Keycloak 26.7.5 did.
  - **The alternative, Keycloak Organizations** (26): one user can belong to several organizations. Checked on 26.7.5: a user joined two, and a token asked for with scope `organization:alpha` carried `organization: ["alpha"]`. The guide covers this under "Managing organizations" and "Mapping organization claims". Using them would replace our single `tenant_id` claim with a chosen organization, which is an ADR-017 change, so it is not adopted now.
- **Resend invitation:** `POST /identity/users/{id}/resend-invitation` (If-Match, `user.update`), allowed only while the user is Invited, before their first sign-in.
- **The invitation link's lifespan:** 72 hours, sent as `lifespan` on `execute-actions-email` and set by `Identity:Keycloak:InvitationLifespan`. Keycloak's own default for admin-sent links is 12 hours (checked: `actionTokenGeneratedByAdminLifespan` is 43200).
- **A user with no roles signs in but can do nothing:** tested. `/identity/me` answers with no permissions, and a staff endpoint answers 403.
- **One choice differs from the addendum:**
  - **The addendum** says CONFIGURE_TOTP is sent "when such a role is first assigned".
  - **The conflict:** IAM-003 refuses that assignment to a user without MFA, so that moment never comes.
  - **Built instead:** the provisioning request's `mfaEnrolled` means "set up a one-time code". The invitation then includes CONFIGURE_TOTP, which Keycloak enforces before the first sign-in completes, so the user may later be given roles that require MFA.

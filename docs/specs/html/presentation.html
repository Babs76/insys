Executive summary §1.1 · §1.3

One platform, three insurance businesses

An enterprise insurance administration platform serving multiple insurers and intermediaries. Domains share common capabilities but keep ownership of their own policies, claims, rules and lifecycles.

The design aim

Platform-level reuse without forcing fundamentally different insurance processes into one generic business model.

Shared across domains
CustomersProduct configurationPricingWorkflowBillingPaymentsAccountingDocumentsIntegrationReportingSecurity

Business objectives

  • Reduce time and cost to launch insurance products
  • Let multiple insurers operate securely on one platform
  • Support many products, countries, currencies and channels
  • Reduce duplicated functionality across lines
  • Keep Life, General and Health processes independent
  • Cover the full policy lifecycle, quotation to termination
  • Handle claims through adjudication, settlement and reconciliation
  • Make pricing and underwriting auditable and reproducible
  • Integrate banks, payers, brokers, providers, reinsurers, accounting
  • Add future products without a platform redesign

Multi-tenancy §4.1 · §4.2

Isolation is enforced at every layer

Strict tenant isolation is a foundational requirement, not an optional feature. It is implemented through multiple independent controls. Select a layer.

Important. Application-level tenant filtering alone is insufficient. Database controls and other independent safeguards should limit the consequences of application defects.

Tenant requirements

TEN-001
Every tenant has a unique, immutable identifier.
TEN-002
Tenant context comes from trusted authentication or service identity, never from an untrusted request parameter alone.
TEN-003

TEN-004

TEN-006
Product configurations, permissions, integration credentials and settings stay isolated.
TEN-007
Administrative access to tenant data is controlled, audited and purpose-restricted.
TEN-008
Onboarding, suspension, reactivation and termination follow defined workflows.
Tenant isolation profiles §4.3

Three profiles, chosen by risk and tier

Strict isolation does not require separate physical infrastructure for every tenant. It requires demonstrable enforcement of boundaries, with stronger options where justified.

Final architectural recommendations §33 · Conclusion

Five decisions that should guide engineering

Treat tenant isolation as a platform capability

Establish trusted tenant context, authorisation, data-access enforcement and isolation testing in the foundation, not per module.

Keep the insurance domains separate

Own policy aggregates, state machines and claims adjudication per domain. Avoid a generic policy model that grows with exceptions.

Build the Product Factory early

Version and govern definitions, rates, benefits, rules, workflows and templates from day one. Retrofitting governance is costly.

Establish financial traceability from the beginning

Trace every premium, commission, payment, refund and recovery to its source. Use explicit reversals, never silent edits.

Deliver one complete journey before expanding

Prove a full vertical slice for the first domain, from product configuration to accounting and reporting, then expand.

The platform in one line

A shared insurance technology platform with three independent insurance domains, not three separate systems joined by a common database.
